The buddy
#########

The screen
**********

The layout is computed from the display's resolution at start. The title
row shows the pet's name (and
its owner's, once the desktop pushed one) and the link: the advertised
Bluetooth name while waiting, ``paired`` once a desktop is connected,
``Claude`` with a tick while heartbeats arrive. The pet lives below it; the
HUD under the pet shows the last transcript lines, or the approval panel
while a tool call waits, or the clock when nothing is happening and the
desktop has synced the time. The status row counts the sessions and shows
the level and the current species.

Moods
*****

The pet derives its mood from the desktop's heartbeat:

=============  ==============================================
State          Trigger
=============  ==============================================
``sleep``      no desktop connected, and the first 12 s after a wake
``idle``       connected, nothing urgent
``busy``       three or more sessions running
``attention``  an approval is pending (the LED blinks)
``celebrate``  a level up (every 50K tokens) or a completed session
``dizzy``      the pet was shaken (swipe it)
``heart``      an approval within 5 s
=============  ==============================================

With the time known and nothing running, the clock face takes over and the
mood follows the hour: asleep at night, hearts at lunch and on weekends,
a celebration on Friday afternoons.

Any display
***********

On a portrait display the HUD sits under the pet, on a landscape one
beside it (the pet takes up to 60 % of the width). The ASCII canvas
(160x88) and the GIFs are shown at the largest whole pixel scale that
fits, so they stay crisp; the fonts and the spacing shrink on displays
smaller than 300 px on a side. The ``native_sim`` build can try other
sizes with the overlays in ``app/overlays/``:

.. code-block:: sh

   west build -b native_sim/native/64 -d build/land zbuddy/app -- \
       -DZEPHYR_EXTRA_MODULES=$PWD/zbuddy \
       -DEXTRA_DTC_OVERLAY_FILE=overlays/native_sim_480x320.overlay

``native_sim_240x320``, ``native_sim_480x320`` and ``native_sim_800x480``
are provided.

Controls
********

===============================  ==========================================
Control                          Action
===============================  ==========================================
tap the pet                      next screen (home, pet, info); on a prompt: approve
hold the pet or tap the menu     open the menu (in a menu: activate the
icon                             highlighted item)
swipe the pet                    shake it (dizzy)
tap a pet/info page              next page
Approve / Deny                   answer the pending tool call
===============================  ==========================================

Menu: ``settings``, ``nap`` (the screen goes dark until the next touch and
the pet's energy refills), ``help``, ``about``, ``demo`` (fake scenarios
every 8 s), ``close``. Settings: ``sound``, ``led``, ``transcript`` (the
HUD), ``ascii pet`` (cycle through the species and the installed pack),
``reset`` (``delete char`` removes a pushed pack and restores the built-in
crab; ``factory reset`` also clears the names, the stats, the preferences
and the Bluetooth bonds; both ask twice).

Pets
****

Three kinds of pet:

- **The built-in crab.** ``characters/clawd`` is generated by
  ``tools/mkpack.py`` and compiled into the firmware
  (``CONFIG_ZBUDDY_APP_BUILTIN_CHARACTER``), so the device has a face
  before anything is pushed.
- **Character packs.** Drag a pack folder onto the desktop app's Hardware
  Buddy window and it streams it over Bluetooth; the device installs it on
  its LittleFS partition and switches to it live. A pack is a folder with
  ``manifest.json`` and 96 px wide GIFs:

  .. code-block:: json

     {
       "name": "clawd",
       "colors": {"body": "#DA7756", "bg": "#000000", "text": "#FFFFFF",
                  "textDim": "#808080", "ink": "#100C0C"},
       "states": {
         "sleep": "sleep.gif",
         "idle": ["idle_0.gif", "idle_1.gif"],
         "busy": "busy.gif",
         "attention": "attention.gif",
         "celebrate": "celebrate.gif",
         "dizzy": "dizzy.gif",
         "heart": "heart.gif"
       }
     }

  A state is one file or a list; a list rotates every five seconds. A
  single GIF plays once and holds its last frame. ``"mode": "text"`` with
  ``{"frames": [...], "delay": 200}`` per state animates short strings
  instead. ``gifsicle --lossy=80 -O3 --colors 64`` keeps packs small; the
  desktop caps them at 1.8 MB.
- **ASCII species.** The eighteen of the original device (capybara, duck,
  goose, blob, cat, dragon, octopus, owl, penguin, turtle, snail, ghost,
  axolotl, cactus, robot, rabbit, mushroom, chonk) plus clawd, each with
  seven animations, drawn on a 160x88 text canvas shown doubled.

Stats
*****

The pet page shows what the buddy has learned about you:

- **mood** (four hearts): the median seconds you take to approve sets the
  base; denying more than a third of the prompts drags it down.
- **fed** (ten pips): progress to the next level; 50K output tokens per
  level, counted from the desktop's cumulative total, so a desktop restart
  does not credit twice.
- **energy** (five cells): 3 at boot, 5 after a nap, one less every two
  hours.
- approvals, denials, time napped, tokens in total and today.

Stats are saved on the events that change them (an approval, a denial, a
level up, the end of a nap), never on a timer, so the flash is not worn by
the heartbeats.

native_sim options
******************

.. code-block:: sh

   zephyr.exe --flash=buddy.bin [--flash_rm] \
       [--shot=<file.ppm>] [--shot-delay=<ms>] \
       [--species=<n>] [--state=<name>] [--screen=<name>] [--page=<n>] \
       [--demo] [--script=<file>]

``--shot`` writes the screen as a binary PPM after ``--shot-delay``
(1500 ms). ``--species`` picks an ASCII species, ``--state`` forces a
mood, ``--screen`` opens ``home``, ``pet``, ``info``, ``menu``,
``settings``, ``reset`` or ``passkey``, ``--page`` the page of it.
``--script`` feeds a file of ``<ms> <json>`` lines as if the desktop had
sent them when the uptime reaches ``<ms>``; ``tools/scenarios/`` has
examples. The pictures in this documentation were made that way:

.. code-block:: sh

   zephyr.exe --flash=buddy.bin --script=tools/scenarios/approval.txt \
       --shot=approval.ppm --shot-delay=3000
   magick approval.ppm approval.png
